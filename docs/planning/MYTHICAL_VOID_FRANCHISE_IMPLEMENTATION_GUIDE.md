# Mythical Void Franchise Implementation Guide

**Status:** Approved direction, implementation in progress  
**Current title:** Mythical Void  
**Current setting:** 2026  
**Audience:** Ages 9-16, with enough mechanical and narrative depth for adults  
**Purpose:** Define what the current game must build now so later titles can
continue the same creature, relationship, equipment, evidence, and history.

---

## 1. Product Direction

Mythical Void is the first chapter of one chronological saga. Its endings must
not create three incompatible universes.

The current working chronology is:

1. **Crashfall:** The current game. Wanderer-77 crashes, the astronaut becomes
   the first human trusted by a creature, discovers the Current, and learns that
   Project Beacon's extraction assumptions are dangerously incomplete.
2. **Remain and Defend:** The astronaut stays long enough to help restore the
   Fend, protect its communities, learn its cultures, and make any departure
   ethically and practically possible.
3. **Secret Homecoming:** Wanderer-77 returns covertly with one informed,
   willing creature. The Sensei's dojo becomes a safehouse. The pair explores
   Earth, investigates Project Beacon, and eventually decides how to report the
   astronaut's survival and landing to NASA without exposing the Fend.
4. **Open First Contact:** Only after the Fend has been defended and the secret
   Earth return has tested humanity does the astronaut attempt open contact.
   This becomes the bridge toward the 2050 Sensei AI setting.

This sequence preserves player agency by allowing different priorities,
methods, relationships, and evidence outcomes inside each chapter. It does not
require separate canonical universes.

---

## 2. Franchise Promise

The player's creature is not replaced between games. Its identity is the
continuing emotional and mechanical center of the saga.

Later titles should be able to inherit:

- player-given creature name;
- procedural identity and visual appearance;
- generated portrait provenance;
- species, rarity, affinity, traits, and signature powers;
- bond, trust, shared-history milestones, and learned behavior;
- restored Current nodes and discoveries;
- Project Beacon reports and disclosure decisions;
- the Earth-forged katana and creature-tech upgrades;
- ship systems, equipment, and major mission outcomes.

No player account identity, age, location, voice, email, or arbitrary personal
free text belongs in the franchise handoff. The bounded creature name is the
only player-authored text and must be validated again before any future
cross-title cloud transfer.

## 3. Current-Game Narrative Contract

### What the player must understand

By the end of Mythical Void, a player should be able to explain:

- Wanderer-77 came from Earth on Project Beacon in 2026.
- Earth is in a genuine crisis, but the mission's extraction model is flawed.
- The astronaut was sent to find energy, minerals, biological samples, and
  signs of life.
- Earth has no live awareness of the Current or the intelligent creature
  communities.
- Living Signals are parts of a planetary communication and life-support
  network, not messages from Earth.
- Extracting from the network weakens habitats and living beings.
- The creature chose to help the astronaut and developed trust through play.
- The creature is intelligent, powerful, and connected to a larger community.
- Restoring Wanderer-77 also creates the danger of revealing the Fend.
- The astronaut cannot ethically leave immediately after repairing the ship.

### What the current game should not claim

- It should not complete the journey to Earth.
- It should not show successful open first contact.
- It should not imply that the creature has been collected or recruited.
- It should not imply that Earth is entirely evil or beyond saving.
- It should not weaken the creature merely to make later gameplay convenient.
- It should not make NASA or every scientist part of the extraction conspiracy.

### Finale correction

The finale is a **priority decision** after a shared canonical
outcome: the coordinates remain protected and the astronaut stays for the next
phase of recovery.

Candidate player priorities:

1. **Defend first:** commit resources to communities and planetary recovery.
2. **Prepare homecoming:** quietly preserve a return route and ask whether the
   companion would ever choose to travel.
3. **Prepare honest contact:** build an evidence standard and consent protocol
   for contact that may happen later.

These priorities alter dialogue, available projects, trust, and legacy data.
They do not split the franchise chronology.

## 4. Creature Power Doctrine

Creatures should feel extremely powerful in every setting.

Earth does not reduce their strength. It changes the consequences of using it.

Every major power needs four authored properties:

| Property | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Capability | The extraordinary thing the creature can actually do |
| Control | How precisely it can use the power without unintended effects |
| Visibility | How detectable or publicly impossible the effect appears |
| Relationship | Whether the creature chooses to use it in this situation |

On the Fend, powers can interact freely with the Current. On Earth:

- strong use creates a detectable resonance signature;
- uncontrolled use can damage human infrastructure or reveal the creature;
- public use can increase exposure;
- deliberate restraint is an intelligent choice, not power loss;
- emergencies can justify spectacular, memorable power reveals.

### Power architecture for the current game

Each creature should have:

- **one universal sense:** detect Current activity, danger, and living intent;
- **one affinity power:** derived from its procedural cosmic affinity;
- **one protective response:** an autonomous emergency ability;
- **one learned partnership move:** unlocked through trust and joint action;
- **one high-power reveal:** a rare authored moment that demonstrates its true
  potential before the game ends.

Examples:

| Affinity | Fend expression | Later Earth expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mist | Hide a route or rescue the pair | Conceal from cameras or blanket a district |
| Crystal | Read, redirect, and amplify resonance | Disable or overload artificial Beacon technology |
| Star | Illuminate pathways and release energy | Power a rescue or create a highly detectable flare |
| Root | Heal habitats and communicate through life | Restore damaged ecosystems or sense living distress |
| Void | Cross barriers and contain unstable space | Bypass secure structures or stop a catastrophic breach |
| Aurora | Shield, stabilize, and redirect force | Protect civilians from machinery, storms, or collapse |

The current power and ability systems should be consolidated around this
doctrine instead of adding another unrelated ability inventory.

## 5. Customer Value and Resonance

The target is a child-safe game for everyone, not a game that speaks down to
children.

### Ages 9-12

Prioritize:

- immediate, readable objectives;
- obvious creature reactions;
- spectacular powers with simple controls;
- visible healing of damaged places;
- exploration, secrets, and collection with clear purpose;
- short story pages and limited required reading.

### Ages 13-16

Prioritize:

- mastery and ability combinations;
- identity through a genuinely unique creature;
- friendship versus duty;
- distrust that must be evaluated rather than automatically rewarded;
- meaningful evidence and disclosure choices;
- mysteries that reward attention.

### Adults

Prioritize:

- coherent science-fiction rules;
- institutional pressure without cartoon morality;
- ecological and economic consequences;
- the astronaut and Sensei relationship;
- humor grounded in character;
- persistent outcomes rather than cosmetic choices.

### Features with the strongest cross-age value

1. A companion whose behavior, abilities, and memories are visibly individual.
2. A world that changes when the player restores or damages it.
3. Powers that solve traversal, rescue, combat, and social problems.
4. A home/community that grows because of the player's actions.
5. Equipment that visibly combines Earth and creature technology.
6. Major generated-art moments that preserve the exact creature identity.
7. Optional mysteries, numbers, codes, and environmental Easter eggs.
8. A story that remains hopeful because people and technology can make better
   choices.

## 6. Nostalgia Without Manipulation

The nostalgia target is the feeling of discovering a strange game world,
raising a unique companion, finding secrets, and carrying a save-file history
forward.

Use:

- expressive pixel gameplay with cinematic portrait reveals;
- compact world maps and memorable biome identities;
- hidden rooms, rare encounters, and sequence-breaking discoveries;
- field logs and black-box fragments;
- creature behavior that continues without becoming an idle-game obligation;
- dojo kata, ship rituals, and recurring musical motifs;
- the numbers 23 and 77 as optional Easter eggs;
- selective red, black, white, and green groupings in Project Beacon, Sensei,
  and legacy interfaces;
- New Game+ and legacy records.

Do not use:

- punitive daily streaks;
- happiness loss because the player stopped playing;
- threats that the creature will suffer unless the player returns;
- loot-box rarity pressure;
- false scarcity around creature welfare;
- nostalgia that copies another game's protected characters or presentation.

## 7. Current-Game Feature Backlog

### A. The Current must become a playable system

Required:

- canonical Current node states: Living, Fading, Severed, and Restored;
- node health visibly changes flora, lighting, ambient life, audio, resources,
  and creature behavior;
- observe, protect, redirect, siphon, and restore interactions;
- at least one reversible early extraction action;
- later evidence showing why previously abundant regions became sparse;
- a world-health summary that uses environmental evidence, not only meters.

Customer value:

- visible consequence;
- exploration meaning;
- a clear ecological lesson learned through action.

### B. Creatures need agency and power demonstrations

Required:

- limited perception and attention;
- one readable current motivation;
- autonomous reactions to danger, signals, settlements, and player choices;
- affinity powers integrated into levels;
- refusal or hesitation with legible reasons;
- partnership moves unlocked through trust;
- a high-power authored rescue before the finale;
- a power-history record for the legacy capsule.

Customer value:

- perceived aliveness;
- power fantasy;
- a creature that feels like a partner rather than a collectible.

### C. The Fend needs communities, not only signals

Required:

- at least one small settlement that the player can revisit;
- named creature roles rather than a large generic population;
- one community project with visible construction or recovery stages;
- requests that use creature powers and astronaut engineering together;
- cultural behavior, rituals, humor, and disagreement;
- a clear reason the astronaut is needed after Wanderer-77 is repaired.

Customer value:

- belonging;
- visible contribution;
- a concrete foundation for Remain and Defend.

### D. Project Beacon needs evidence and institutional nuance

Required:

- delayed or stored mission packets, never convenient live instructions;
- Earth-side voices with different motives;
- NASA/scientific mission records separated from corporate extraction orders;
- a dual-record dossier comparing Earth classification with field observation;
- recoverable black-box fragments explaining the mission's changes;
- an evidence quality score based on discoveries, not a morality score;
- no final transmission until the campaign finale.

Customer value:

- mystery;
- story comprehension;
- legitimate debate rather than a simplistic villain.

### E. Wanderer-77 must support later secret travel

Required seeds:

- damaged stealth/descent systems;
- a secure navigation core containing the return vector;
- a manual landing capability;
- a black-box recorder that can prove the astronaut survived;
- limited capacity for one willing passenger;
- life-support incompatibilities that creature technology later solves;
- the risk that activating the long-range uplink exposes the Fend.

The current game repairs or discovers these systems. It does not perform the
secret Earth landing.

### F. The Sensei must exist before Homecoming

Required:

- a pre-mission memory or recorded message establishing their relationship;
- martial-arts humor in astronaut field notes;
- one remembered lesson that becomes a gameplay mechanic;
- the Earth-forged katana as a personal and cultural object;
- an encrypted personal contact path discovered late in the campaign;
- no successful real-time contact during the current game.

The Homecoming opening can later use the encrypted path to arrange the secret
landing and dojo safehouse without inventing the relationship retrospectively.

### G. The katana must be visible, useful, and continuous

Required:

- field-kit reveal;
- inventory and artifact timeline;
- visible baseline use before upgrades;
- Resonant Edge and Aurora Guard changes reflected in art and gameplay;
- non-lethal utility functions: cutting debris, redirecting energy, deflecting
  projectiles, opening routes, and stabilizing machinery;
- upgrade provenance in the legacy capsule.

Customer value:

- mastery;
- visible progression;
- an iconic object connecting Earth, the Fend, and the Sensei.

### H. Generated art should mark identity and history

Required:

- one living portrait after naming when eligible and available;
- deterministic prompt inputs from creature genetics and traits;
- an immediate authored fallback with no broken-image state;
- updated portraits at major lifecycle or power milestones;
- katana art at baseline and upgrade milestones;
- opt-in, server-protected generation only;
- no required AI request during gameplay;
- private owned storage and provenance;
- under-16 local authored alternatives in the current policy.

Later story video should be built from approved identity assets and authored
storyboards. It is not a launch dependency.

## 8. Homecoming Requirements Seeded Now

The current game should preserve enough information to support this future
opening:

1. The astronaut completes Remain and Defend.
2. The companion independently chooses to see Earth.
3. The pair restores Wanderer-77's concealed descent capability.
4. The Homecoming opening begins with the astronaut using the previously seeded
   encrypted personal channel to contact the Sensei.
5. The Sensei authenticates the message through shared history and prepares a
   secret landing site and the dojo safehouse.
6. The landing avoids Project Beacon tracking but leaves a recoverable trail.
7. NASA eventually learns the astronaut survived and receives a controlled
   account of the landing and discoveries.
8. The Fend's location and intelligent life remain protected until the later
   Open First Contact chapter.

The current game must establish the Sensei, the contact path, the future
passenger decision boundary, and the ship capability. It must not assume the
companion agrees to travel, or show the successful call or secret landing.
Those are playable events in Homecoming after Remain and Defend.

### Future Homecoming gameplay pillars

- compact Earth locations rather than a simulated open-world planet;
- dojo preparation and relationship hub;
- public concealment and civilian blending;
- investigation and evidence recovery;
- non-lethal katana action;
- spectacular creature powers used selectively;
- Exposure, Trust, and Evidence as persistent pressures;
- environmental rescues that show Earth's beauty and why it is worth saving;
- Earth experienced through the creature's curiosity.

Candidate Earth level functions:

| Level function | Player value | Story purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hidden landing zone | Stealth, navigation, first Earth reaction | Proves the return was deliberate and secret |
| Sensei dojo | Safehouse, training, equipment, relationships | Connects martial tradition to ethical technology |
| Wild Earth biome | Traversal, rescue, creature wonder | Shows why Earth is beautiful and worth protecting |
| Dense public district | Concealment, humor, non-lethal problem solving | Makes extraordinary powers costly to reveal |
| Beacon recovery site | Investigation, katana utility, evidence | Connects the Fend mission to Earth-side decisions |
| NASA return sequence | Controlled disclosure and difficult testimony | Confirms survival without forcing open contact |

Homecoming's core pressures should be:

- **Exposure:** who has evidence that the creature exists;
- **Trust:** the companion, Sensei, selected humans, and institutions;
- **Evidence:** what can be proved without revealing the Fend;
- **Control:** how precisely extreme creature powers can be used;
- **Wonder:** what the creature learns to value about Earth.

NASA is not a final boss. Returning to NASA is a controlled disclosure problem:
the astronaut must report survival, the landing site, mission failure, and
bounded discoveries while deciding what evidence can be released safely. The
creature remains a willing partner with veto power over disclosure.

These are sequel requirements. The current repository should store compatible
data and narrative seeds, not attempt to build Earth levels now.

### Current-game changes that make Homecoming possible

Every sequel seed must also improve Mythical Void for a player who never buys
another game.

| Current-game feature | Player value now | Franchise value later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fend Current Archive | Explains Living Signals, companion histories, and discoveries in-world | Becomes evidence the astronaut can compare with Earth records |
| Kinship Beacon | Makes the first Fusion lineage permanently change the Sanctuary | Carries family history across visiting, swapping, and later titles |
| Complete companion record | Preserves name, powers, bond, portraits, choices, and lineage | Lets the same willing companion enter Homecoming |
| Sensei memories and `DOJO-23-77` | Gives Wanderer-77 personality, humor, and a human relationship | Authenticates the opening secret contact without retroactive exposition |
| Ship capability board | Turns repairs into visible, useful progression | Supplies stealth descent, manual landing, black-box proof, and one safe passenger place |
| Companion consent moments | Shows intelligence, refusal, and trust during current play | Makes travel to Earth an informed choice rather than collection |
| Evidence dossier | Clarifies science, government pressure, and extraction sponsorship | Supports the controlled NASA return without exposing the Fend |
| Affinity power history | Gives each companion spectacular and mechanically distinct moments | Preserves full power on Earth while adding exposure consequences |
| Earth-forged katana history | Makes upgrades visible and useful in expeditions | Connects the astronaut, Sensei, Earth materials, and creature technology |
| Priority finale | Gives the current game a complete ethical conclusion | Establishes Remain and Defend before Secret Homecoming and Open First Contact |

The current game must **not** place a live call to the Sensei, land on Earth,
report to NASA, or decide that a companion will travel. It must prove the
relationship, recover the encrypted route, preserve the necessary ship and
evidence state, and let the companion's future consent remain open. The first
successful contact, secret landing, Earth exploration, dojo safehouse, and
controlled NASA disclosure remain playable Homecoming events.

### New player-facing progression

The intended progression is:

1. **One signal:** hatch and identify a companion through play, not rarity
   alone.
2. **Two signals:** a dormant Fend kinship record responds. Both companions
   remain secure and the Fusion Pod becomes an authored mystery.
3. **Stable Pod:** field level and adult lifecycle requirements make the
   protocol operational.
4. **First lineage:** the Kinship Beacon lights permanently in the Sanctuary.
5. **Shared history:** powers, lineage, portraits, Current restoration, katana
   upgrades, and community trust accumulate into one readable record.
6. **Final priority:** the player decides what Wanderer-77 prepares first after
   protecting the coordinates, without prematurely splitting the chronology.

This progression combines creature-raising nostalgia with modern persistence:
the surprise of a new companion remains, but no existing companion is falsely
threatened, consumed, or discarded to create urgency.

## 9. Technical Architecture

### Campaign legacy capsule

The first implementation is a versioned, local, privacy-minimized contract:

```text
story.projectBeacon.legacyCapsule
  schemaVersion
  sagaId
  sourceChapter
  nextChapter
  intent
  recordedAt
  companion
  relationship
  discoveries
  equipment
  campaign
```

Rules:

- schema changes are versioned;
- no direct player identity fields;
- identifiers and lists are bounded and normalized;
- generated media stores provenance, not secret provider credentials;
- the capsule is rebuilt at major campaign milestones;
- cloud transfer remains optional;
- later titles must migrate old capsule versions;
- gameplay remains complete if no capsule is imported.

### Fend community record

The current game now uses one ordered, save-backed settlement contract:

```text
world.fendCommunity
  schemaVersion
  builtProjectIds
  contributionHistory
  foundedAt
  lastContributionAt
```

The four projects are:

1. **First Light Shelter:** earned by tending the garden and restoring the
   first region; grants one additional expedition heart.
2. **Current Well:** earned through broader restoration and care actions;
   grants one additional crystal charge.
3. **Wayfinder Relay:** earned through five restored regions and all three
   Living Signal observations; blocks one hit per expedition.
4. **Living Commons:** earned only after all six regions are restored, the
   companion's extreme rescue is witnessed, and the Wanderer-77 uplink is held.

This is not a spendable currency or construction grind. A project acknowledges
verified care, observation, and restoration already performed by the player.
Operations are ordered, idempotent, bounded, and suitable for later cloud
reconciliation.

### Fend resident and cooperative-request record

Community relationships now use a separate portable contract:

```text
world.fendResidents
  schemaVersion
  metResidentIds
  activeRequestId
  activeRequestBaseline
  completedRequestIds
  history
  firstMetAt
  lastInteractionAt
```

The first four authored residents are:

1. **Kiri, Trailkeeper:** calibrates the First Light Shelter without blocking
   the Current beneath it.
2. **Mara, Current Listener:** returns loose energy through the Current Well
   instead of treating it as stored fuel.
3. **Tovan, Wayfinder:** joins all three Living Signals into a warning network
   that carries guidance rather than orders.
4. **Ilyra, Commons Keeper:** establishes that coordinates remain held and that
   the companion is a citizen, not a sample.

Only known IDs, bounded evidence snapshots, and completed operation records are
stored. Dialogue, player identity, and arbitrary free text are excluded. One
request can be active at a time, repeated operations are idempotent, and a
new post-acceptance cooperative action is required where repeating existing
evidence would create grind. The record is carried into the current campaign
legacy schema version thirteen so Remain and Defend can continue the same
relationships.

### Fend culture and First Listening record

The completed Living Commons now unlocks one bounded cultural decision:

```text
world.fendCulture
  schemaVersion
  firstListening
    status
    heldAt
    operationId
    selectedPriority
  history
```

The First Listening follows a Fend rule: no one speaks twice until every voice
has been heard. Kiri argues for refuge, Mara for restoration, and Tovan for a
larger warning network. Ilyra asks the player and companion to choose what
begins first. The choice does not mark another need as wrong and does not
branch the ending. It establishes a community priority that later missions can
honor.

Only a known priority ID, bounded operation ID, and timestamps enter the save.
All resident and companion responses remain authored in code. The selected
priority appears in the Commons, Project Beacon, resident return dialogue, and
campaign legacy schema version thirteen.

### Companion-led Earth-boundary record

The completed First Listening unlocks a companion-led review at Wanderer-77:

```text
story.projectBeacon.companionConsent
  schemaVersion
  activeCompanionId
  records
    companionId
    travelStatus
    disclosureStatus
    locationBoundary
    informedRisks
    willingPassenger
    vetoRecognized
    powerBoundary
    reviewedTopicIds
    history
```

Each companion has an isolated record. Switching companions cannot copy travel
or disclosure boundaries between them. The first review establishes:

1. Fend coordinates remain withheld.
2. Earth may learn that Wanderer-77 survived, but a creature is not evidence
   the astronaut can offer.
3. A companion conceals extreme power when possible but chooses to save a life
   when secrecy and survival conflict.

Completion records informed boundaries and a recognized veto. It deliberately
leaves `willingPassenger` unset and travel in `decision_deferred`; an available
seat is not an invitation or consent. Bounded operation IDs, per-companion
history, normalization, and replay rejection make the record suitable for
later cloud reconciliation without storing arbitrary dialogue or player
identity.

### Sensei memory and remembered-lesson record

The astronaut's relationship with the future Sensei now develops through
three ordered, save-backed memories at Wanderer-77:

```text
story.projectBeacon.sensei.memoryLedger
  schemaVersion
  recalledMemoryIds
  lesson
    id
    status
    practiceCount
    firstPracticedAt
    lastPracticedAt
  history
```

The memories are recalled only after current-game evidence gives them meaning:

1. **The Dojo Floor:** field-kit recovery and the first Project Beacon record
   recall the lesson, "When the world moves, begin with your footing."
2. **The Open Door:** the first expedition or a witnessed autonomous rescue
   recalls that trust begins with how a person enters.
3. **The Empty Hand:** protecting or redirecting the Current recalls that power
   includes what a person chooses to leave unharmed.

The first memory unlocks **Centering Stance**. After a real non-lethal hit, the
player can release movement on solid ground for 1.25 seconds. Once per
expedition, Wanderer-77's suit reseals one damaged integrity segment. The
mechanic uses the existing movement, health, mobile-control, HUD, achievement,
and save contracts; it does not add another screen button or replace a
companion's autonomous rescue.

The memory history stores only known memory IDs, a bounded operation history,
lesson practice counts, level IDs, timestamps, and the witnessing companion
ID. It stores no dialogue or player identity. Ordered normalization repairs
noncontiguous imports, duplicate operations are rejected, and the record enters
campaign legacy schema version thirteen.

No live message is sent. `DOJO-23-77` remains a recovered but unused encrypted
route. Secret Homecoming can later authenticate its opening contact from the
three played memories and practiced lesson instead of relying on retroactive
exposition.

### Wanderer-77 ship and protected-evidence record

The ship is now a playable review hub after the first expedition instead of a
container for hidden sequel flags:

```text
story.projectBeacon.shipArchive
  schemaVersion
  reviewedSectionIds
  firstReviewedAt
  completedAt
  history
```

The review has three ordered sections:

1. **Return systems:** recovered living-world systems, concealed descent,
   sealed navigation, black-box proof, passenger support, and the held uplink.
2. **Evidence:** the astronaut's survival, the Current, intelligent life, and
   witnessed extreme power, with protected findings distinguished from
   reportable proof.
3. **Boundaries:** Fend coordinates, companion disclosure, travel, and Earth
   contact.

The board unlocks only after the field kit and opening mission record have
been recovered and at least one expedition finding exists. It repeatedly
states that no transmission has been sent, coordinates remain sealed, and
travel remains undecided. A physical passenger place is recorded as a ship
capability, never as a companion invitation or consent decision. Intelligent
life is a person and witness, not evidence owned by the astronaut.

Only known section IDs, stable operation IDs, bounded timestamps, and the
witnessing companion ID enter the save. Imports repair noncontiguous review
state, remove unknown fields, reject replay, and retain no arbitrary player
text. New Game+ replays this campaign discovery while the legacy capsule
preserves the previous chapter's review in schema version thirteen.

### Portable companion identity and shared-history record

The Creature Profile now provides one player-facing archive that makes each
companion's identity and shared history legible:

```text
creature.identityArchive
  schemaVersion
  creatureId
  reviewedChapterIds
  firstReviewedAt
  completedAt
  history
```

The four ordered chapters are:

1. **Identity:** stable companion identity, player-given name, species,
   rarity, affinity, growth stage, and lineage.
2. **Living Form:** the best local generated portrait when one exists, or a
   species-matched authored study, plus the artwork's provenance.
3. **Shared Journey:** bond, care, expedition, rescue, power, Sensei-memory,
   and ship-review evidence witnessed by this companion.
4. **Inheritance:** lineage, katana adaptations witnessed together, the First
   Listening priority, and a versioned sequel handoff.

The portable record never contains a generated-image URL, account identity,
free-form notes, dialogue, exact location, or hidden prompt. Generated artwork
remains a local presentation asset because provider URLs may expire and should
not become identity credentials. The only player-authored field is the bounded
companion name. Everything else is a known identifier or a bounded value
derived from authoritative game state.

Collection switching keeps a distinct archive for each stable companion ID.
Old imports repair noncontiguous chapter history and discard unknown fields.
Campaign legacy schema version thirteen carries the bounded record so a later game
can recognize the same companion, relationships, powers, lineage, memories,
and witnessed equipment without presuming travel or disclosure consent.

### Canonical systems to consolidate

The repository currently has overlapping creature skills, secret abilities,
genetics, bond slots, birth abilities, agent behavior, and level-specific
power code.

Before expanding powers:

1. Define one canonical `CreaturePowerProfile`.
2. Map genetics and unlocked abilities into it.
3. Let levels query capabilities through one interface.
4. Keep simulation state authoritative.
5. Let AI-generated media describe existing state, never invent powers.

### Portable creature and Fusion contract

The Fusion Pod is not a detached collection minigame. It is the first place
where the game proves that creature identity, consent, family history, and
abilities can survive beyond one scene and eventually beyond one device.

Build now:

- globally unique creature and fusion-operation identifiers;
- a versioned lineage record with parent identifiers, generation, origin, and
  creation time;
- atomic fusion commits so twins are both recorded or neither is;
- an interrupted-fusion recovery path that cancels safely before commit;
- one canonical cooldown and history record;
- full creature-state switching that preserves bond, powers, portraits,
  lifecycle, and lineage;
- bounded, privacy-minimized records suitable for optional cloud sync;
- a future ownership boundary that can support consent-based creature visiting,
  swapping, or breeding without treating creatures as anonymous inventory.

Do not build open trading or multiplayer matchmaking yet. Those systems require
server-authoritative ownership, replay protection, moderation, age-aware social
design, and explicit transfer consent. The current game should establish clean
portable records without exposing a public exchange.

The local transaction protects against partial twin creation and duplicate
commits. It stages the exact generated genetics, rarity, birth events, and
abilities before the reveal, so a local refresh resumes the same lineage
rather than rerolling it.

Before synthesis, the player now reviews one explicit local-sanctuary consent
record. Both companions must be mature, present, and well enough to approach
willingly. The record confirms that neither parent is traded, consumed, or
replaced. New Game+ preserves unresolved reconciliation and consent records.

Cloud-online Fusion reserves ownership and capacity in Supabase. The browser
sends at most two candidate identities; the locked server reservation selects
whether one or two signatures stabilize, then protected Edge Functions
generate the outcome and atomically commit the named lineage. The browser
cannot write the operation table or call internal execution/finalization RPCs.

Local-only play retains deterministic generation. If cloud-enabled play must
finish offline, the completed lineage receives a bounded reconciliation
receipt. Cloud upload and another Fusion remain blocked until that exact
receipt is reserved, executed, and finalized through the authority service.

Shared Fusion now opens one deliberately narrow cross-owner boundary:

- it is available only to 16+ profiles that explicitly enable Cloud Save;
- one keeper creates a private, expiring code and gives it directly to a
  second known keeper;
- each keeper selects one willing adult or elder companion;
- both keepers separately confirm the bounded companion signal;
- the server creates two linked siblings atomically, one permanently assigned
  to each Sanctuary;
- each keeper names only the sibling entering their own Sanctuary;
