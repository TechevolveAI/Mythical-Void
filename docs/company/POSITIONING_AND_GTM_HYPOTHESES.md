# Positioning and Go-to-Market Hypotheses

**Status:** Research hypotheses, not approved strategy  
**Date:** 11 August 2026

## Recommended starting position

> **Mythical Void is a living science-fiction creature adventure where a
> one-of-one alien companion remembers how you care for it, helps restore a
> threatened world, and changes what getting home means.**

This is stronger than leading with “AI game” or “procedural graphics.” Those
are enabling technologies, not the emotional reason to play. AI should become
proof that the companion and world feel responsive, distinctive, and alive.

### Message hierarchy

1. **Emotional promise:** meet a companion that feels like yours alone.
2. **Player activity:** explore and restore six living realms together.
3. **Narrative tension:** Earth sent you to find resources; the world you find
   is alive.
4. **Agency:** your care, discoveries, and choices shape the relationship and
   what happens next.
5. **Access:** free in a browser, with no download or account required to start.
6. **Trust:** family-friendly, local-first, and designed around care rather
   than extraction.

### Supporting lines to test

- “Your creature. Your choices. A world that remembers.”
- “The first alien life to trust you is waiting.”
- “You were sent to save Earth. First, decide what saving means.”
- “No two companions. No two bonds. No two journeys.”

These are test variants, not a license to claim capabilities that are not
visible in the current build.

## Audience architecture

The product currently signals a younger core audience but has narrative and
systems that may appeal beyond it. Treat the market as connected roles rather
than one generic persona.

| Role | Initial hypothesis | What they need to believe | Best evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core player | Creature-loving players roughly 8–14 | The companion is expressive; play is understandable and rewarding | A real hatch-to-bond gameplay moment |
| Trust gate | Parents/guardians | The game is safe, respectful, worthwhile, and transparent | Plain-language safety, privacy, and gameplay proof |
| Adjacent player | Teens/adults who enjoy creature, cozy, and narrative games | The systems and story have enough depth to avoid feeling infantile | Choice, progression, world, and companion-memory proof |
| Amplifier | Family-friendly, creature-game, cozy-game, and narrative-game creators | Their audience will get a distinctive, watchable experience | A concise creator build and story hooks |
| Industry amplifier | Indie game, procedural art, animation, and responsible-AI communities | Mythical demonstrates a credible new production/player model | Technical craft backed by shipped footage and honest limitations |
| Partner | Platforms, publishers, family brands, education/culture partners | The product has audience fit, governance, and delivery reliability | Cohort evidence, trust package, roadmap, and team capability |

### Strategic decision to validate

Should launch communications lead with the child/family market or with the
broader creature/narrative market?

**Working recommendation:** keep the product safe for the younger core while
initial discovery targets the adults who control accounts, communities,
coverage, and permission. Do not use behavioral targeting toward children.
Validate whether older creature-game players engage naturally before narrowing
the brand into “a children's game.”

## Category and differentiation

### Category

Use familiar language first: **browser creature-raising action-adventure**.
Then introduce the new layer: a **living companion and choice-driven science
fiction world**.

### Differentiation stack

1. One-of-one procedural creature identity.
2. A companion relationship expressed through care, memory, agency, and shared
   adventure.
3. Restoration as both a world mechanic and ethical story premise.
4. A complete mission arc rather than an isolated virtual-pet loop.
5. Immediate browser access with no account gate.
6. Responsible AI and child-safety architecture treated as product design, not
   a disclaimer.

The stack is defensible only when each layer is easy to experience and show.
The go-to-market system should expose real moments, not describe an invisible
future.

## Go-to-market sequence

### Phase 1: evidence-led early access

Goal: learn which audience/message reaches meaningful play and where the first
session breaks.

- Fix technical findability and establish a search baseline.
- Define a lawful, minimal activation measurement plan.
- Run 5–10 moderated sessions across the intended age bands, with appropriate
  guardian involvement.
- Create three short proof assets: hatch/bond, realm restoration, and the
  Project Beacon choice.
- Invite a small set of well-matched creators or communities only after the
  first-session experience and support path are ready.
- Publish a visible “what changed because of players” log.

### Phase 2: coherent public update

Goal: prove one repeatable awareness-to-meaningful-play path.

- Anchor the campaign to a genuine product milestone.
- Use one message, three proof moments, and audience-specific context.
- Route every channel to tagged landing paths or links.
- Measure play starts, activation, return, qualitative response, and technical
  health—not impressions alone.
- Have support, incident, and rollback owners ready before launch.

### Phase 3: distribution and commercial discovery

Goal: expand reach and validate how Mythical becomes a durable business.

- Package a browser-portal and creator-ready build where technically suitable.
- Test platform, publisher, brand, education, and cultural partnerships against
  explicit fit criteria.
- Research willingness to pay using concepts and interviews before introducing
  purchases.
- Keep player trust and game integrity as monetization guardrails.

## Growth loops to test

### Companion identity loop

Distinct companion -> emotionally meaningful moment -> player/parent chooses to
share -> new person discovers the hatch experience.

Guardrails: sharing must be voluntary, age-appropriate, privacy-preserving,
provenance-aware, and must not expose a child identity or create social
pressure.

### Restoration story loop

Striking damaged/restored realm -> short before/after proof -> audience asks
about the world -> story content sends them to play the relevant journey.

### Build-in-public learning loop

Real design/AI/safety problem -> transparent explanation -> expert/creator
discussion -> improved system -> credible update.

The subject is the craft and outcome. Avoid performative “AI made everything”
claims.

### Feedback trust loop

Player/parent evidence -> visible decision -> product improvement -> contributor
is told what changed -> higher-quality future participation.

## Channel priority hypothesis

| Priority | Channel | Purpose | Start condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Owned website/search | Canonical explanation and conversion | Technical indexing plus measurement plan |
| 1 | Moderated research/playtests | Product truth and audience language | Consent, safeguarding, script, and note handling |
| 1 | Selected creators | Trust transfer and observable play | Stable first session and creator/support kit |
| 2 | YouTube/short video | Demonstrate living moments | Repeatable capture and approved voice |
| 2 | Relevant communities | Conversation and learning | Human participation and community-rule review |
| 2 | Indie/AI/craft press | Differentiated studio story | Shipped proof and accurate technical claims |
| 3 | Email/lifecycle | Return and update communication | Lawful consent model and clear value exchange |
| 3 | Paid acquisition | Scale a proven message | Activation measurement and strict spend cap |
| 3 | Broad autonomous social | Efficient distribution | Brand evaluations, approvals, and incident controls |

## First experiment portfolio

1. **Positioning comprehension:** show the existing page and three message
   variants to intended players and parents. Measure accurate unaided recall,
   desire to play, concerns, and expected game type.
2. **First meaningful moment:** observe whether players reach and understand
   the first companion bond without facilitation.
3. **Proof-asset test:** compare hatch/bond, restoration, and mission-choice
   clips with small matched audiences. Measure qualified click-to-play and
   post-play response where lawful—not view count alone.
4. **Parent trust test:** ask parents to find and explain data, AI, cost, combat,
   and contact policies using the site. Record confusion and missing evidence.
5. **Branded discovery baseline:** verify indexing, submit the sitemap, and
   monitor whether owned pages become the canonical brand result.

## Explicit non-goals for the first cycle

- high-volume autonomous posting;
- child-targeted behavioral advertising;
- public chat, follower mechanics, or open user-generated content;
- a complex CRM before consent and lifecycle value are clear;
- monetization optimization before audience and retention evidence;
- presenting simulated agent audiences as market validation;
- claiming the studio or game is autonomous where a human review step exists.
