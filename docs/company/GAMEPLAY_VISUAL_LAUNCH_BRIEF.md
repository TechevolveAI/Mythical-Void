# The four moments Mythical Void needs before a visual launch

The old screenshots and videos are not being reused. They came from the real game, but they did not help a person understand why the game is exciting. In the platforming capture, the creature can look like a small green square beside the astronaut. Phone controls, instructions and test-state clutter take over the frame. The video shows an arrival screen rather than a compelling stretch of play.

This is now a game-readiness problem, not a marketing-editing problem. Cropping, captions or AI artwork cannot honestly fix it.

The corrected 27 August capture proves the technical faults can be removed: the real creature stays visible, both characters remain separate, the phone frame is complete and the videos meet their duration and frame checks. A complete review of all 300 captured frames still rejected all four moments. The scenes remain too dark, flat and diagram-like, with small labels and no strong visual beginning, event and payoff. The next work is therefore better playable staging and art direction—not another edit of the same material.

## What the game needs to make visible

### 1. The creature helps

Show the player and creature facing one obvious problem together. The creature performs a recognisable action and the result appears in the same shot. A nine-year-old should be able to point at the frame and explain what the creature did.

### 2. A choice changes something

Show one choice in plain words, then the visible consequence: a place changes, a creature responds, a route opens or a relationship shifts. The consequence matters more than the menu.

### 3. A genuinely strange discovery

Choose one feature that could only belong to Mythical Void: unusual life, matter, weather, gravity, memory or dimensional behaviour. Keep the player and creature visible so the image still reads as a game rather than background art.

### 4. Movement feels alive

Record a short, uninterrupted piece of normal play. The creature must remain fully rendered and recognisable throughout. No square placeholder, missing sprite, menu interruption, dead pause or explanatory edit is allowed.

## Minimum visual bar inside the game

- The creature's body—not its empty texture area—must be recognisable at phone size.
- The astronaut and creature must not overlap or become visually confused.
- Important action must remain inside a clean central frame on phone and desktop.
- Instructions should be short enough that they do not cover the action.
- The capture state must use a real creature profile and the same renderer a player receives.
- One screenshot should communicate one moment. It should not need a paragraph to rescue it.

## How the studio will capture it

New capture runs go into `.visual-review/candidates/`, which is not part of the website. Automation may prepare candidates and reject obvious faults. It cannot approve them. An adult watches every frame and records the decision. Kevin approves the exact asset, wording and channel before anything is posted outside the owned website.

The machine-readable shot list and current status live in `docs/company/content/visual-launch-moments.json`.
