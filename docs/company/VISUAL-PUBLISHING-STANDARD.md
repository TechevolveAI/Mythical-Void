# Mythical Void visual publishing standard

The old rule was too weak: if a capture came from the real build and passed technical checks, it could be presented publicly. That produced honest but confusing and unattractive material.

The new rule is simple: a public visual must be real, understandable and worth looking at.

## What a good capture must show

1. One clear moment. A person should understand the situation before reading the caption.
2. A visible creature. It must be fully rendered and large enough to recognise on a phone.
3. Real play. Show movement, discovery, choice, cooperation, danger or consequence—not a menu standing in for gameplay.
4. Deliberate framing. Keep the player, creature and the important part of the world in one readable composition.
5. A reason to care. The frame should create curiosity, tension, surprise, delight or an obvious question.

## Automatic rejection

Reject the whole asset if any frame contains a missing creature, square placeholder, broken texture, clipped subject, unreadable instruction, debug state, unexplained empty space or long dead moment.

Also reject a technically clean capture when the selected moment is dull, crowded or needs a paragraph to make sense.

## Human review

Automation may capture and check files. It may not approve them.

Before release, a named adult reviewer must:

- watch every frame of every video;
- inspect stills at their real public size, including a phone-sized view;
- confirm that the creature remains visible and correctly rendered;
- confirm that a child or parent can say what is happening;
- confirm that the caption matches what the visual truly shows;
- record approve or reject in `public/press/visual-publication-register.json`.

If there is doubt, do not publish.

## First replacement shot list

- Creature helps the player through one clear obstacle.
- Player makes a visible choice and the world responds.
- Player and creature discover one strange, memorable feature of a realm.
- Short movement sequence with the creature continuously visible and no menu interruption.

The game build must be capable of producing these moments before the marketing workflow can produce a credible gameplay pack.
