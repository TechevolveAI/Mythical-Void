# The First Five playtest

**State:** held; the creature’s first impression is not ready

## Why this comes before promotion

Before Mythical Void asks a game platform or a large audience for attention,
five adults who do not know the project should be able to start it, recognise
the creature, understand what to do and reach a moment that makes them want to
continue.

This is a twelve-minute product check, not market research and not a claim that
five people represent the whole audience. It exists to catch obvious confusion
before it is multiplied by promotion.

## Who takes part

- five adults aged 18 or over who have not previously played or worked on
  Mythical Void;
- three use a desktop or laptop and two use a phone;
- nobody under 18 is invited;
- no employee, close collaborator or person already familiar with the game is
  counted in the five.

Kevin can invite suitable adults personally after approving the exact message
in [the invitation and scorecard](FIRST_FIVE_INVITATION_AND_SCORECARD_2026-08-31.md).
There is no public recruitment post, mailing list, advertising or automated
follow-up.

## What we say

> I am testing whether a free browser game makes sense to somebody seeing it
> for the first time. This takes about twelve minutes. We are testing the game, not you.
> Nothing is recorded, you can stop at any time, and I will not write
> down your name, contact details, creature name or anything personal.

## The twelve-minute session

### Minute 0–1 — first look

Open `https://mythicalvoid.com/` in a fresh private browser window. Ask:

- What do you think this is?
- What looks most interesting?
- What would you press next?

Do not explain the game.

### Minute 1–7 — play without coaching

Ask the adult to press Play and continue for up to six minutes. The observer
does not give instructions unless the participant is completely stuck and asks
for help. Note only:

- did the game load;
- could they begin without help;
- could they point to the creature;
- what they thought the first goal was;
- the first place they became confused;
- whether they reached one meaningful action;
- whether phone controls and layout remained usable.

### Minute 7–10 — plain questions

- What happened?
- What did the creature do?
- What do you think you should do next?
- What was the best-looking moment?
- Was there a moment that looked broken, crowded or unfinished?
- Would you continue playing: yes, maybe or no?

### Minute 10–12 — one recommendation question

> Who, if anyone, would you recommend this game to—and what would you tell them
> it is?

Record a short paraphrase, not a name, quotation or personal story.

## What may be recorded

Use only participant codes `A1` to `A5` and these fixed fields:

- desktop/laptop or phone;
- load succeeded: yes/no;
- began without help: yes/no;
- creature unmistakable: yes/no;
- first meaningful action reached: under one minute, one-to-two minutes,
  two-to-six minutes, or not reached;
- next action clear: yes/no;
- phone usable: yes/no/not applicable;
- wants to continue: yes/maybe/no;
- one short de-identified confusion note;
- one short de-identified recommendation description.

Do not record names, contact details, exact ages, exact device details, faces,
voices, video, audio, screens, creature names, save data, locations or account
information. Do not copy anything from the participant’s browser.

## The release rule

The First Five gate passes only when:

- all five loads succeed;
- at least four begin without help;
- at least four can unmistakably identify the creature;
- at least four reach a meaningful action within six minutes;
- at least four know what to do next;
- both phone sessions remain usable;
- at least three say they want to continue;
- no repeated severe safety, trust or presentation problem appears.

These are practical release gates, not audience percentages. A serious problem
can fail the run even if the count passes. Fix the game and run a new five;
never delete or rewrite the earlier result to make it look successful.
