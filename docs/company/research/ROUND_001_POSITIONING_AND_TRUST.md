# Research Round 001 — Positioning, Trust, and First Value

**Status:** Ready for adult recruitment after Kevin approves the research
purpose and outreach  
**Collection:** Not started  
**Owner:** Unassigned  
**Product:** Current public storefront and production game

## 1. Decision served

Choose the first public positioning lead and identify the most important trust
or first-session barrier before Mythical scales discovery.

This round does not estimate market size or prove retention. It answers:

1. What game do people believe Mythical Void is before playing?
2. Which promise—companion, living-world story, or feature breadth—creates the
   clearest qualified desire to play?
3. Can adults responsible for younger players find and accurately explain cost,
   accounts, data, AI, combat, and contact?
4. Does the first playable experience deliver the promise participants chose?

## 2. Staged sample

### Round 001A — adults only, ready first

- 6 parents/guardians of children broadly within the intended player range;
- 4 adult creature-game, cozy-game, or narrative-adventure players;
- mix of mobile and desktop habits where practical;
- do not recruit employees, close collaborators, or people already familiar
  with the product into the main sample.

This is a purposeful qualitative sample. Results are directional and must not
be reported as percentages of the market.

### Round 001B — younger players, gated

Only after Mythical names a safeguarding owner/backup and approves participant
information, guardian permission, child assent, moderator training, recording,
retention, compensation, withdrawal, and incident procedures:

- 2 participants aged 9–12;
- 2 aged 13–15;
- 2 aged 16–17;
- guardian present or available under the approved protocol.

Do not collect exact birth dates in the shared research record. Recruitment
and contact data remain in a separate restricted system.

## 3. Hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Evidence that supports it | Evidence that weakens it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RH-001 | Companion-led positioning produces the clearest accurate expectation and strongest qualified intent | Participants recall relationship, describe actual play accurately, and want to reach the hatch/bond moment | They expect a chat toy, pet simulator only, or capabilities not present |
| RH-002 | Story-led positioning differentiates the game without making it sound text-heavy | Participants recall Earth/living-world tension and still expect active play | They expect a visual novel or cannot explain what they do |
| RH-003 | Current trust information lets parents make an informed first-play decision | Parents find and accurately explain all six trust topics without moderator help | They incorrectly state what data/AI/accounts/cost involve or cannot find answers |
| RH-004 | The first playable experience pays off the chosen promise | Post-play description matches pre-play expectation and names a concrete meaningful moment | Promise feels absent, delayed, confusing, or contradicted |

## 4. Message cards

Show one card per participant first, using balanced assignment. Do not show all
three and ask for a preference until the unaided comprehension task is complete.

### M-A — current brand lead

> **Your creature. Your journey.**  
> Bond with an alien companion, restore six living realms, and decide what home
> means in a free browser adventure.

### M-B — companion lead

> **A companion shaped by the journey you share.**  
> Hatch an alien creature, learn how it responds to your care and choices, and
> explore a living world together.

### M-C — story lead

> **You were sent to help Earth. The world you found was alive.**  
> Restore six threatened realms beside an alien companion—and decide what
> Project Beacon should become.

All three are hypotheses. M-B must not be shortened to a sentience or unlimited
memory claim.

## 5. Session plan — 45 to 55 minutes

### Welcome and context — 5 minutes

- Use the [Research Session Protocol](../templates/RESEARCH_SESSION_PROTOCOL.md).
- Explain that the game, site, and wording are being tested—not the participant.
- Confirm recording state and voluntary withdrawal.

### Positioning comprehension — 7 minutes

Show the assigned message card for up to 20 seconds, then remove it.

- What do you think this is?
- What would you do in it?
- Who does it seem intended for?
- What, if anything, makes you want to try it?
- What might stop you?

Record unaided words before asking rating questions.

### Storefront comprehension — 8 minutes

Open the current storefront. Ask the participant to explain:

- game type and main activity;
- cost and whether an account/download is required;
- what is distinctive;
- current release status;
- any capability they think uses AI.

Do not correct them until the task ends.

### Parent trust tasks — 8 minutes

For parents/guardians only: “Imagine you are deciding whether a younger player
can try this today.” Ask them to find and explain:

1. price/payments;
2. account requirement;
3. where progress/data are stored;
4. AI or external-service behavior;
5. combat/content suitability;
6. who to contact with a concern.

Record task success, time/confusion, what evidence was trusted, and what remains
missing. The moderator must not interpret policy as legal advice.

### First-play observation — 15 to 20 minutes

Ask the participant to start without instruction and think aloud only when
comfortable. Observe:

- successful load and input choice;
- understanding of mission and role;
- age/privacy setting comprehension;
- hatch initiation and completion;
- first description of the creature;
- evidence of bond, memory, or agency they independently notice;
- confusion, assistance, technical failure, and voluntary stopping point.

Do not collect or copy the creature name into the shared research record.

### Debrief — 7 minutes

- What was the most memorable moment?
- What did the companion seem able to do?
- What did you expect that did not happen?
- What would you do next?
- What would stop you returning or recommending it?
- After playing, which of the three message cards is most honest and why?

## 6. Observation rubric

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Category comprehension | Wrong/unclear category | Partly accurate | Accurate activity and format |
| Differentiation recall | None or generic | One vague distinction | Concrete truthful distinction |
| Trust task | Incorrect/unfindable | Found with confusion | Found and accurately explained |
| Start success | Cannot begin | Begins with help/friction | Begins independently |
| Companion payoff | No meaningful evidence noticed | Prompted/vague evidence | Independently names concrete behavior/moment |
| Next-step clarity | Does not know what to do | General idea | Clear intended next action |

Scores organize observation; participant words and behavior remain the
evidence. Do not average these into a market KPI.

## 7. Decision rules

- Recommend a message lead only if it is accurately understood, does not create
  a material false expectation, and the play experience supplies visible proof.
- If no message passes, fix product/storefront clarity before scaling reach.
- Any repeated trust misunderstanding becomes a P0 company issue even if play
  intent is high.
- Any severe safety/privacy issue exits ordinary research synthesis and follows
  the restricted runbook.
- A problem observed across both parents and adjacent adult players has higher
  source diversity, not automatically higher prevalence.
