# Mythical Void creature concept artist brief

**Status:** prepared for Kevin’s approval; no artist contacted and no money approved  
**Purpose:** find one lovable, unmistakably alien visual language that can grow
into a whole universe of creatures

## The short version

Mythical Void is a browser adventure for children, teenagers and families. A
human astronaut is stranded in another dimension and meets a living creature.
The creature is not a pet, a robot helper or a talking human in disguise. It is
the player’s first proof that this universe has different kinds of life and
different rules of matter.

The game can already generate many creature identities, traits and colours. The
current pictures do not live up to that idea. They often look like mascots,
ordinary animals, blobs, portals, insects, plants or symbols. We need the visual
foundation before we produce more variations.

The first commission is not “draw lots of creatures.” It is:

> Find one clear visual language for life from another dimension—strange enough
> to feel new, warm enough for a child to care about, and readable in a small
> browser-game frame.

## What the player should feel

In the first three seconds:

- **What is that?** It should create curiosity before explanation.
- **It is alive.** Its weight, attention and movement should make sense.
- **I want to help it.** It should have emotional presence without becoming a
  cute Earth animal.
- **I have never seen this exact thing before.** Its strangeness should come
  from how it exists, not from extra horns, eyes or glowing decorations.

The overall feeling is wonder, intelligence, vulnerability and hidden power—not
horror, ugliness, comedy or a toy mascot.

## Where the alien ideas can come from

Please think beyond animal parts. Explore life shaped by physical rules that do
not exist on Earth:

- pressure that travels through a body like a tide;
- liquid that remembers its last shape;
- crystal tissue that bends gravity rather than reflecting light;
- a body partly folded into another depth;
- living material that borrows matter from the ground and returns it;
- sound held as visible weight;
- warmth, memory or direction carried through connected tissue;
- an organism whose “down” changes before it moves;
- attention shown through rhythm, orientation or pressure rather than a face.

These are starting points, not a menu of effects. Each idea must create one
connected living body with a believable way to sense, move and touch the world.

## What to avoid

Reject a direction if its strongest first reading is:

- cat, dog, fox, rabbit, bird, fish, snake, crocodile, insect or jellyfish;
- human, baby, superhero, armour or clothing;
- dragon, familiar fantasy monster or stitched-together animal;
- plant, mushroom, coral, rock pile or crystal ornament;
- portal, eye, badge, logo, map marker, vehicle, weapon or furniture;
- round mascot, smiling blob, floating head or toy;
- “alien” created mainly by purple glow, stars, tentacles or lots of eyes.

Nature can inspire materials and behaviour. It must not supply the whole body
plan.

## Non-negotiable design rules

1. **One organism:** the complete silhouette reads as one connected living
   thing, even in solid black.
2. **Clear attention:** the player can tell what it notices without needing a
   normal face or human expression.
3. **Believable movement:** the design shows where force begins, how it travels
   through the body and where the creature meets the ground or air.
4. **Small-screen clarity:** it works at roughly 96 pixels wide inside a
   390-pixel phone view without a label, ring or glow outline.
5. **Lovable without being a pet:** care comes from behaviour, vulnerability,
   rhythm and recognition—not puppy eyes or baby proportions.
6. **Room for generations:** the design language can create meaningful
   differences in structure, material, movement and realm influence without
   becoming random decoration.
7. **Same identity everywhere:** reveal art, normal gameplay and later story art
   must still look like the same individual.

## The three exploration directions

The first page of work should explore all three. They are different questions,
not three finished species.

### 1. Living current

A connected organism whose form is held together by travelling pressure. It
moves because a wave of force passes through it, not because legs run or a blob
bounces.

### 2. Memory matter

A connected organism whose tissue keeps part of its previous position or shape
for a moment. It should feel physical and present, not like a ghost trail or
duplicate image.

### 3. Private gravity

A connected organism that changes its own direction of weight. Its body settles
toward a new “down” before the whole creature moves. It must not read as a ball,
drone, floating rock or flower.

## First delivery: the decision sheet

Before colour, detail or rendering, provide:

- twelve small black silhouettes: four per exploration direction;
- the same twelve silhouettes shown at 96 pixels wide;
- no labels beside the silhouettes;
- a separate key explaining the physical idea only after the label-free sheet;
- one sentence for how each form senses, moves and touches the world;
- a short list of any visual references and the right to use each reference.

Kevin selects no more than three. If none feels alive, alien and appealing
without explanation, the work stops at this stage. Do not polish rejected
shapes.

## Second delivery: one chosen creature

Only after Kevin selects a direction:

- one clean side view and one three-quarter view;
- solid-black and greyscale checks;
- a restrained material and colour study in three game realms;
- five clear moments: rest, notice, gather force, contact and recover;
- one ordinary phone-sized game mock-up beside the astronaut;
- a simple map of the body layers needed for movement;
- one page showing how three related identities could differ without changing
  the design language.

This is still private concept work. It does not enter the game until a person
approves the ordinary phone and desktop views.

## Rights and tool use

- Record who made every source, reference and final file.
- Record whether work is human-drawn, AI-assisted or mixed.
- If generative tools support exploration, record the provider, model, prompt,
  date and source references. Generated output is concept material until it is
  reviewed, cleaned and rights-checked.
- Do not imitate a living artist, game, film character, brand or protected
  creature design.
- Do not use a child’s name, exact age, photograph, voice or private material.
- No generated concept may be shown publicly as gameplay.

## Approval and stop points

- Software may check file sizes, missing layers, clipping and whether the
  silhouette survives at phone size.
- Software may reject obvious failures. It may never approve beauty, appeal or
  originality.
- Kevin approves the visual direction.
- A separate adult reviewer approves the first real gameplay frames.
- Spending, artist contact, contracts, publication and platform use each need
  separate approval.

## Success

This brief succeeds when one creature can appear in a small, ordinary game frame
and a person can honestly say:

> I do not need the label to know it is alive. I cannot reduce it to an animal or
> object. I want to see what it does next.
