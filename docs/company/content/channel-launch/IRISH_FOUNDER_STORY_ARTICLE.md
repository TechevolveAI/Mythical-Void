# What a father-and-son game taught me about using AI without handing over responsibility

*By Kevin Murphy, founder of Mythical Void*

Mythical Void began at home in Ireland with my nine-year-old son and a simple question: what could we make if we combined his imagination with the new generation of creative AI tools?

We were not trying to write a business plan. We were imagining creatures from somewhere beyond our own world: how they might look, what strange rules could shape them, and what kind of place they might call home. A child can move from a glowing egg to a liquid organism to a being made from gravity without stopping to ask whether the idea fits a familiar category. That freedom became the heart of the project.

Those conversations slowly became something playable. Mythical Void is now a free browser adventure. The player begins as the pilot of Wanderer-77, a mission sent from Earth in search of hope. The ship crashes in another dimension. The player finds and hatches an alien creature, explores six damaged realms, fights the corruption holding their guardians, and eventually decides what Project Beacon should become.

No download or account is needed to begin. That matters to us because curiosity should not have to pass through a complicated sign-up screen before it can start.

## AI helped us explore more possibilities

Generative AI made it possible for a very small project to explore ideas at a scale that would once have been out of reach. It helped us try visual directions, question weak writing, organise large amounts of work, test variations and keep moving when the number of tasks became larger than one person could comfortably hold in their head.

But “AI helped” is very different from “AI made it”.

Tools can produce a lot of answers quickly. They cannot decide which answer belongs in this world. They do not know what will delight a nine-year-old, what will confuse a parent, what promise the game can honestly support, or when an idea is impressive but wrong for the story. Those decisions still need taste, care and responsibility.

We have thrown away plenty of AI-assisted work. Some pictures looked spectacular but had little connection to the game. Some words sounded clever but were difficult for a young player to understand. Some ideas suggested a feature was deeper than the playable version really was. Moving quickly only helps if someone is willing to stop, look carefully and say no.

## The child’s imagination leads; the child’s privacy stays protected

The father-and-son beginning is true and important, but it does not turn a child into a marketing asset.

I can share that my son was nine when the project began and that his imagination helped open the door. I will not share his name, school, routine, private accounts or identifying details. He does not need to appear in public or answer strangers’ questions for the story to be real.

That boundary has shaped how we think about the wider studio. Mythical Void is a game that children and teenagers may enjoy, but public messages, feedback and partnerships must be handled by adults. We do not privately contact children or invite them to send personal information to the studio.

## A fantasy game can still open a door to real science

The game also contains optional discovery moments using selected public information from NASA. The adventure remains fictional, but a real image or space clue can lead to a new question: what could live under conditions completely unlike Earth?

We developed a free four-page STEM Creature Lab around that idea. A young designer starts with a real observation, clearly separates it from fiction, invents an organism for an impossible environment, and writes a prediction that could be tested. It is made for ages 9-14, families, clubs and classrooms, with adults leading for younger designers.

Mythical Void is independent. NASA does not endorse the game or the activity. Keeping that sentence visible is part of treating both science and families with respect.

## AI-first should not mean nobody is responsible

I want Mythical Void to become an AI-first independent studio. For me, that means using AI and automation wherever they genuinely help a small team imagine, organise, test and learn. It does not mean asking software to quietly run a company with nobody answerable for the result.

People must remain responsible for the story, safety, evidence, money, publication and any conversation involving children or families. Drafting can be automated. A public promise cannot be. Research can be assisted. A partnership cannot be invented. A hundred visual ideas can be generated. Only work that represents the real game should be shown as gameplay.

That may sound like a slower way to use fast tools. In practice, it is what makes speed useful. Clear boundaries let us automate ordinary work without losing sight of who must make the important decision.

## What comes next

Mythical Void is already playable, but it is still growing. We want to make the creature universe stranger, the restoration of each realm more visible, and the consequences of Project Beacon more meaningful. We also want to learn how a very small studio can use new tools without hiding their use or handing away its judgement.

The game started with a child asking what might be possible. The studio is being built around a second question: how much can these tools help us create while people remain clearly responsible for what we put into the world?
